# Generator budget failure: diagnosis and fix

The original generator sampled dense layouts at every requested size. Larger runs have factorially many ordered digit tuples; generation could spend its per-puzzle solver budget before finding two solutions, so disagreement-guided repair never ran. Even after finding ambiguity, repeated one-digit clue repairs could consume all attempts without producing a unique board. A reproduced 12×12, seed-42 request exhausted the full 20 seconds and returned no puzzle.

Sizes 7–12 now use a connected band of overlapping 2×2 blocks with randomized rotation/reflection. It spans all requested rows/columns and has runs of length two or three. This keeps the real solution counter and ambiguity repair tractable without changing the selected grid size or adding given digits. Sizes 5–6 retain their existing sampled layouts.

Repair mode reserves the smaller of one second or 25% of its total budget for recovery. If no rated unique puzzle was found, it constructs a fresh short-run witness using a randomized orientation and an extremal three-color palette, derives its clues, and runs the same complete solution counter. The formula constructs a legal fill; it is not a stored solution and does not establish uniqueness by itself. A second solve produces the real human-technique rating. If either verification or rating cannot finish in the remaining time, no unverified puzzle is returned. The response identifies this recovery as `method: constructive`; a requested difficulty mismatch remains explicit.

The attempt cap applies to random attempts; constructive recovery is at most one additional attempt within the same overall wall-time budget. Random-regeneration experiment mode does not silently use recovery. The original recorded paired experiment tables and calibration data remain historical evidence for the earlier policy; they have not been relabeled as measurements of this fix. New experiments should record the policy and generation method.

Regression tests cover every selectable size, forced coloring failure, successful verified recovery and refusal to claim uniqueness when counting hits its limit. Separate tests re-count returned puzzles and check the returned fills against all constraints. Exact requested difficulty is still best-effort, and empirical calibration for sizes 7–12 remains provisional.

After the fix, all 80 requests at seeds 0–9 across sizes 5–12 returned verified unique puzzles within three seconds. Three used constructive recovery. A separate seed-42 check matched the independent CP-SAT oracle at all eight sizes. The restarted live API returned valid unique puzzles at all eight sizes in 21–743 ms; the 12×12 request took 743 ms. These are recorded regression observations, not a universal latency guarantee. The full suites passed 345 backend and 13 frontend tests; the production build passed.

The final post-restart audit repeated the 80-request sample: 80/80 succeeded, four used recovery, and the maximum elapsed time was 2271 ms under the same three-second cap. Different recovery counts can occur because a wall-time cap depends on machine load. This newer sample is in `results/final-audit.json`; the earlier three-recovery sample remains in `results/generator-regression.json`. The final audit changed documentation/evidence only, not the generator algorithm.
